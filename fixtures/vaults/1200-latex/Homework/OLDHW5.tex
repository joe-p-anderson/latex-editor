\begin{questions}
    \question \textbf{Hydraulics}: Consider a hydraulic system where the control end of the system is attached to a narrow tube (area 4 cm$^2$) and the working end is attached to a thick tube (area 100 cm$^2$).
    \begin{parts}
        \part If the control end is pushed in a distance of 50 cm, how far will the working end be pushed out? Explain your reasoning
        \part If you are pushing the control end by applying a 20 N force, how much force is applied at the working end? Explain your reasoning.
        \part Show that when the control cylinder is compressed by 50 cm with a force of 20 N, the work done at the control cylinder is equal to the work done by the working end.
        \part Explain why this is true in general: any force applied over any distance will transfer that work to the other end of the cylinder.
        \part Why is a hydraulic system useful if all it does is transfer work?
    \end{parts}

    \question \textbf{Pascal's principle and dams}: Suppose a dam is placed across a river that is 75 m wide and 12 m deep.
    \begin{parts}
        \part Explain why dams of this sort are generally thicker near the base than at the top.
        \part How much force does the dam have to supply to hold back the water?
    \end{parts}

    \question \textbf{Pascal's barrel}: Suppose that a certain barrel can support a certain pressure, say 4.5 kPa, before bursting.
    \begin{parts}
        \part How high would the fluid head need to be for the barrel to burst?
        \part How narrow would the straw have to be to accomplish this with only 1 L of water?
    \end{parts}

    \question \textbf{Buoyancy}: Suppose you have made a raft out of 20 pine logs (pine has a density of .5 g/cm$^3$) which have a radius of 6 cm each.
    \begin{parts}
        \part What is the maximum weight your raft could support?
        \part If just you (say you weigh 60 kg) were to be on the raft, how much water would be displaced?
    \end{parts}
\end{questions}
